# Location Guide Quality Template

Use this structure for every permanent location record. A guide may begin as a dynamic record page and later be promoted to an editorial guide, but the route and permanent record ID stay the same.

## Page identity

- One H1 only: `[Location Name] Rockhounding Guide`
- Permanent record ID displayed in the trust/evidence area
- Canonical URL tied to the permanent guide slug
- Primary intent: the exact location, not generic keyword repetition
- State/county/material links are supporting navigation, not substitutes for location-specific content

## 1. Quick answer

Give the visitor the field-planning answer first:

- where the location is
- what may be found
- current collecting/access status
- coordinate precision
- whether the record is Ready, Conditional, Research or Protected
- last verification date

Do not imply collecting permission from a map pin.

## 2. Field status card

Show separate confidence dimensions:

- Geological evidence: High / Medium / Low / Unknown
- Coordinate precision: Exact / Entrance / Area / Context / Withheld
- Access confidence: High / Medium / Low / Unknown
- Collecting-rule confidence: High / Medium / Low / Unknown
- Last checked
- Next review due

## 3. Map and navigation

Show the publishable coordinate only.

Actions:

- Open map
- Copy coordinates
- Center on map
- Find nearby verified locations
- Return to state map

For generalized coordinates, say that the point represents an area/context rather than an exact collecting spot.

## 4. Why this location matters

Write a location-specific explanation grounded in evidence. Examples:

- known geological unit
- historically reported occurrence
- official collecting area
- stream/gravel context
- mine/quarry history
- managed fee-dig operation

Avoid interchangeable filler that could be pasted onto another location.

## 5. What you may find

For each material, distinguish:

- documented occurrence
- plausible geological target
- community observation
- unsupported/legacy claim

Do not turn a mineral occurrence into a promise that visitors will find specimens.

## 6. Access, ownership and collecting rules

Separate:

- land ownership/manager
- public access
- collecting permission
- tool/quantity limits
- permit or fee
- seasonal restrictions
- current closures
- mining-claim/private-property concerns

If any of these are unknown, say so directly.

## 7. Getting there

Use practical, non-invented information:

- nearest public approach/road when verified
- parking/entrance context
- road-surface or vehicle notes
- walking distance where supported
- mobile-signal/navigation caution where supported

Never invent turn-by-turn directions from a coordinate alone.

## 8. Geology and evidence

Explain the relevant geology in plain language, then list the evidence lineage.

Useful fields:

- geologic unit/formation
- rock type
- depositional/igneous/metamorphic context
- mineralization style
- why the target material may occur here

## 9. Field method

Give location-appropriate search guidance, for example:

- float and talus inspection
- gravel-bar scanning
- dry washing/panning only where legal
- surface collecting
- spoil-pile inspection
- beach/shoreline searching
- fossil-bed observation

Never recommend prohibited digging or unsafe mine entry.

## 10. Hazards and field readiness

Only include relevant risks:

- heat/dehydration
- flash flood
- wildfire
- unstable cuts
- tides
- snakes/insects
- remote-road access
- mine workings
- hunting season
- winter conditions

## 11. Best season / timing

Add only when evidence or climate/access context supports it. Do not manufacture a precise best month.

## 12. Evidence and verification history

Show:

- source title/publisher
- what claim each source supports
- date checked
- material changes over time
- status history

A change from Ready to Conditional or Protected should be explainable.

## 13. Nearby alternatives

Recommend geographically nearby records ranked by:

1. usable access status
2. freshness
3. distance
4. coordinate confidence
5. material relevance
6. community evidence

Do not rank by distance alone.

## 14. Public Testimony

Display approved field stories only.

Each story card may show:

- contributor ID
- badge
- optional display name
- visit date
- experience
- finds/observations
- access-condition notes
- approved photos
- helpfulness votes

A visible notice must state that community reports do not override current official access/collecting rules.

## 15. Share Your Story & Photos

Every guide includes a clear button.

Submission accepts:

- permanent Contributor ID, or creates one for a first-time contributor
- private recovery code when reclaiming an ID on another device
- visit date
- story title
- experience
- finds/observations
- access/condition notes
- up to five photos
- optional review flags such as closure, fee change, inaccurate coordinate or new sign

All submissions are Pending until approved by an administrator.

## Editorial quality rule

A guide is not considered high quality because it reaches a word count.

A strong guide is specific, sourced, field-usable, legally cautious and transparent about uncertainty. Many complete guides will naturally fall around 1,200-2,500 words, but short evidence should remain short rather than being padded with repetitive prose.

Dynamic record pages can launch with the minimum trustworthy evidence and be enriched progressively without changing the permanent URL.
