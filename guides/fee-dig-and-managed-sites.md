# Fee-Dig, Gem Mining & Managed Rockhounding Sites in the USA

Fee-dig and managed collecting sites can be useful for beginners, families, travelers and anyone who prefers a destination where access and collecting activity are easier to understand than at an unmarked historic locality.

Two national starting points are:

- [Fee-Dig, Gem Mining & Panning Sites](https://usarockhounding.com/rockhounding-map/fee-dig-sites/)
- [Managed Rockhounding & Collecting Sites](https://usarockhounding.com/rockhounding-map/managed-collecting-sites/)

## Fee-dig vs managed collecting sites

A **fee-dig site** generally involves paid access to search for specified materials under the operator's rules.

A **managed collecting site** is a broader category. It may be operated by a public agency, park, nonprofit, club, local authority or another manager and can have its own access, permit, tool and quantity rules.

Neither category should be treated as permanently open. Always verify current operations before travel.

## What to check before visiting

### Opening dates and hours

Some sites are seasonal, appointment-only, weather-dependent or closed on particular days.

### Fees

Check whether the price is per person, per bucket, per vehicle, by weight or tied to a particular activity.

### What you may keep

Do not assume every material found can be removed. Some sites may have limits or special rules for valuable or uncommon finds.

### Tools

Confirm whether the operator allows hand tools, screens, shovels, hammers, sluices or rented equipment.

### Age and safety rules

Family-oriented sites may still require eye protection, closed-toe shoes or adult supervision.

### Accessibility

Ask about parking distance, terrain, shade, restrooms, water and mobility access if these matter for your group.

### Weather

Mining and collecting areas can become unsafe during extreme heat, storms, flash-flood conditions or freezing weather.

## Why managed sites can be useful for beginners

A well-run managed site can reduce uncertainty around several common beginner questions:

- where the collecting boundary is;
- whether collecting is allowed;
- what material is expected;
- what tools are permitted;
- how much material may be kept;
- where to park and check in.

This does not eliminate the need to read current rules, but it can make a first trip more straightforward.

## How to choose between fee-dig sites

Compare more than price. Consider:

1. target material;
2. natural in-place collecting vs seeded material;
3. amount of material you may keep;
4. travel distance;
5. physical difficulty;
6. equipment included;
7. operating season;
8. whether reservations are required.

## Combine with state research

After finding a managed or fee-dig site, open the exact [state rockhounding guide](https://usarockhounding.com/rockhounding-map/by-state/) to explore other opportunities in the same state.
